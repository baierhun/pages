# **Class Debates: AI and Society**

In this activity, you will participate in **two class debates** exploring different questions about artificial intelligence. You will work in teams to research evidence, develop arguments, respond to opposing points, and persuade the class of your team's position.

Each student will participate in **one debate** and will be responsible for speaking during at least one round of that debate.

These debates will help you practice **critical thinking, public speaking, evaluating evidence, and considering multiple perspectives on artificial intelligence.**

---

## **Debate Topics**

### **Debate 1: Can AI Think?**

**Resolution:**

> **Machines can be intelligent.**

One team will argue **in favor** of the resolution, while the other team will argue **against** it.

You may consider questions such as:

* What does it mean to "think"?
* What does it mean to be "intelligent"?
* Can a machine understand something?
* Is the Turing Test enough to demonstrate intelligence?
* What does the Chinese Room argument tell us about AI?
* How can we know whether another person—or a machine—actually understands something?

### **Debate 2: Is AI More Beneficial Than Harmful to Society?**

**Resolution:**

> **The benefits of artificial intelligence to society outweigh its potential harms.**

One team will argue **in favor** of the resolution, while the other team will argue **against** it.

You may consider both the benefits and potential harms of AI, including:

* Medicine and healthcare
* Education
* Scientific research
* Accessibility
* Productivity and automation
* Job displacement
* Misinformation and deepfakes
* Bias and discrimination
* Privacy
* Creativity and copyright
* Human relationships and dependence on technology
* Other effects of AI on society

Your goal is not simply to list advantages and disadvantages. You must **use evidence and reasoning to argue why the benefits or harms are more significant.**

---

## **Teams**

The class will be divided into **four teams of approximately four students each.**

| Debate       | Team   | Position                                     |
| ------------ | ------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Debate 1** | Team A | **Affirmative:** Machines can be intelligent |
| **Debate 1** | Team B | **Negative:** Machines cannot be intelligent |
| **Debate 2** | Team C | **Affirmative:** Benefits outweigh harms     |
| **Debate 2** | Team D | **Negative:** Harms outweigh benefits        |

Each team is responsible for:

* Researching its assigned position
* Developing **2–3 major arguments**
* Finding reliable evidence to support its arguments
* Anticipating the opposing team's arguments
* Preparing rebuttals
* Dividing speaking responsibilities among team members

**Every student must speak during the debate.**

---

# **Debate Structure**

Each debate will follow the same format.

### **1. Introduction & Setup**

The teacher will:

* Explain the resolution
* Introduce the two teams
* Explain the rules and time limits

Teams will announce their speaker order.

---

### **2. Opening Statements**

**Affirmative Team:** 4 minutes
**Negative Team:** 4 minutes

Each team presents its main arguments.

**Instructions:**

* Present 2–3 key arguments.
* Explain your reasoning clearly.
* Use evidence and examples.
* Do not interrupt the opposing team.

Each member of the team should contribute to the opening statement.

---

### **3. Rebuttal Round**

**Affirmative Team:** 4 minutes
**Negative Team:** 4 minutes

Respond directly to the opposing team's arguments.

**Instructions:**

* Identify weaknesses or problems in the opposing team's reasoning.
* Present counterarguments.
* Use evidence to challenge opposing claims.
* Do not simply repeat your opening statement.

---

### **4. Cross-Examination / Q&A**

**10 minutes total**

The teacher will ask questions to both teams.

**Instructions for Teams:**

* Answer clearly and concisely.
* Defend your position using evidence and reasoning.
* Listen carefully to the question before responding.
* You may build on another team member's answer, but avoid repeating the same point.

**Questions may address:**

* Your team's evidence
* Your assumptions
* Weaknesses in your argument
* Hypothetical situations
* The opposing team's arguments
* Consequences of your position

---

### **5. Closing Statements**

**Affirmative Team:** 3 minutes
**Negative Team:** 3 minutes

Each team should:

* Summarize its strongest arguments.
* Respond to the most important opposing points.
* Explain why its position is ultimately more convincing.
* Leave the audience with a clear final argument.

---

### **6. Class Vote & Reflection**

After the debate, the class will vote on which team presented the **stronger argument**.

You will then complete a brief reflection considering:

* Which arguments were most convincing?
* What evidence was most effective?
* How well did each team respond to opposing arguments?
* Did the debate change or strengthen your own opinion?

---

# **Time Management**

| Segment                 |       Time |
| ----------------------- | ---------: |
| Introduction & Setup    |      5 min |
| Opening Statements      |      8 min |
| Rebuttals               |      8 min |
| Cross-Examination / Q&A |     10 min |
| Closing Statements      |      6 min |
| Class Vote & Reflection |      3 min |
| **Total**               | **40 min** |

# **Tips for a Strong Debate**

### **Build an Argument**

Don't just state your opinion. Explain **why** you believe it.

**Weak:**

> "AI is good for medicine."

**Stronger:**

> "AI can improve medicine because it can analyze large amounts of medical data more quickly than humans, potentially helping doctors identify diseases earlier."

### **Use Evidence**

Support your claims with:

* Reliable news sources
* Scientific research
* Statistics
* Expert opinions
* Real-world examples
* Specific examples of AI systems

### **Anticipate the Other Side**

Before the debate, ask:

> **"If I were on the other team, how would I argue against us?"**

Prepare a response before the debate begins.

### **Listen**

A strong debater doesn't just memorize a speech. Listen carefully to the opposing team so you can identify opportunities for rebuttal.

### **Remember**

The goal is not necessarily to **prove that your position is absolutely correct**. Your goal is to make the **strongest, best-supported argument** you can.
