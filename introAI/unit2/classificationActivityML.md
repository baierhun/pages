# Mystery Classification

**Submissions** Please answer the questions from section `6` in the text box and insert a PDF of your slides presentation

## The Challenge

With a partner, create a **3-category classification system** and use examples to "train" your classmates.

Your category names should be **completely made up** so they give no clue about the categories.

**Example:** Zorp • Blen • Kavo

---

## 1. Create Your Categories

Choose **three categories** with your partner.

<div>

**Your categories should:**

* Have observable characteristics
* Be broad enough to have many examples
* Have lots of images available
* Be different enough to discover patterns

</div>

<div>

**Good examples:**

* Animals
* Food
* Transportation
* Clothing
* Buildings
* Household objects

**Avoid:**

* Specific dog breeds
* Movie characters
* Specific brands
* Phone models
* Very small categories

</div>

</div>

Give each category a **random, meaningless name**.

| Category   | Secret Name |
| ---------- | ----------- |
| Category 1 | __________  |
| Category 2 | __________  |
| Category 3 | __________  |

**Do not tell the class what your categories actually are!**

---

## 2. Create Your Training Data

Find **9 images** that belong to your three categories.

| Category   | Images |
| ---------- | -----: |
| Category 1 |      3 |
| Category 2 |      3 |
| Category 3 |      3 |
| **Total**  | **9**  |

Put the images into a Google Slides presentation.

**Label each image with its category name.**

These 9 examples are your **training data**. Choose examples that show the pattern you want your classmates to recognize. Avoid four nearly identical examples.

---

## 3. Train the Class

Present your 9 training images to the class.

For each image, tell the class its category:

> "This is a Zorp."

> "This is a Blen."

> "This is a Kavo."

**Do not explain what the categories mean.**

Your classmates must figure out the patterns from your examples.

---

<div class="break"></div>

## 4. Create Your Test Data

Find **21 new images**:

| Category   | Test Images |
| ---------- | ----------: |
| Category 1 |          7  |
| Category 2 |          7  |
| Category 3 |          7  |
| **Total**  |      **21** |

**Do not label these images.** Mix all 21 together in your presentation.

Your classmates will classify each image as:

**Zorp • Blen • Kavo**

The test images should be **different from your training images**.

---

## 5. Reveal the Answers

After the class has classified all 21 images, reveal the correct category for each one.

**Correct:** ______ / 21

### Accuracy

**Correct ÷ 21 × 100 = ______ %**

## 6. Questions

# Mystery Classification — Questions

Answer these questions **before presenting your classification system to the class.**

1. **What are the three categories you created, and what characteristics do you expect will help people distinguish between them?**

2. **Why did you choose your 9 training images? What do you hope your examples will teach the class about each category?**

3. **Look at your 21 test images. Which ones do you predict will be the hardest for the class to classify? Why?**


## The Big Idea

You just experienced a simplified version of **machine learning classification**.

| This Activity          | Machine Learning     |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- |
| Training images        | **Training data**    |
| Category names         | **Labels / classes** |
| Patterns you noticed   | **Features**         |
| Classifying new images | **Prediction**       |
| Test images            | **Test data**        |
| Correct predictions    | **Accuracy**         |
